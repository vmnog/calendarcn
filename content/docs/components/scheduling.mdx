Scheduling is Notion Calendar's way to offer someone a few times to meet. Press <Kbd>S</Kbd>, or click **Scheduling** in the sidebar, and mark the times you're free on the day or week grid. The context panel writes them up as a short message, and **Copy** puts it on the clipboard, ready to paste into an email or a chat. Nothing on the calendar changes. In the preview above, press <Kbd>S</Kbd> and drag on a day.

## Marking times

While you mark times, a light hatch covers the grid and the events can't be opened or dragged. A pill at the bottom says "Mark available times on your calendar".

- **Drag** on a day to mark the time you drag over, in steps of `snapMinutes` (15 minutes by default). A **click** marks one meeting length from the slot you click.
- **Drag a marked time** to move it, to another day too, and drag its top or bottom edge to resize it.
- **Remove** one with its <Kbd>×</Kbd>, on the grid or in the panel, or click it and press <Kbd>Delete</Kbd>. A "Time range deleted" toast offers Undo, as in Notion Calendar.
- Times that overlap or touch on the same day become one: 10 to 11 AM and 11 AM to 12 PM read as 10 AM to 12 PM.
- **Escape** or **Cancel** leaves without copying anything.
- On phones and tablets, a long press starts each of these, so a swipe still scrolls. The pill gets Cancel and Copy buttons there, since the context panel isn't on screen.

Starting from the month, year or agenda view switches to the week view. You can move to other weeks while you mark: the times you marked stay.

## The text

The panel shows the meeting length (30 minutes unless you pick another), the text, your time zone and each marked time. **Copy** copies the text and leaves; the copy button beside "Snippet" copies it and lets you keep marking. A toast says "Availability copied to clipboard". The text is worded the way Notion Calendar's is:

| You marked               | The text                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One time                 | Would 30 min anytime tomorrow Wed Nov 18, 11 AM - 1 PM (GMT-3) work for you?                                                                        |
| Several times on one day | Would 30 min during any of these times (all in GMT-3) tomorrow Wed Nov 18 work for you?<br />- 11 AM - 1 PM<br />- 2-3 PM                           |
| Times on several days    | Would 30 min during any of these times (all in GMT-3) work for you?<br />- Tomorrow Wed Nov 18, 11 AM - 1 PM or 2-3 PM<br />- Thu Nov 19, 4-5:30 PM |

Times are in the viewer's time zone, named by its short label ("EST", "GMT-3"). Day and month names come from the provider's `locale`, and the times follow its `hourCycle`: "11:00 - 13:00" on a 24-hour clock. "Today" and "tomorrow" are added to those days, and a day in another year carries the year.

## Installation

Scheduling comes with the items it shows in, so there's nothing extra to install:

- [Calendar Core](/docs/components/calendar-core): `SchedulingProvider`, `useScheduling()`, `useSchedulingShortcuts()` and the text, in `@/lib/availability`.
- [Time Grid](/docs/components/time-grid): the layer for marking times, drawn by the day and week views inside a `SchedulingProvider`.
- [Details Sidebar](/docs/components/details-sidebar): the scheduling panel (`AvailabilityPanel`) and the pill (`AvailabilityHint`).
- [Calendar Sidebar](/docs/components/calendar-sidebar): the Scheduling row.
- [Command Menu](/docs/components/command-menu): "Share availability..." and the scheduling link commands.

## Usage

[Calendar App](/docs/components/calendar-app) has all of it: <Kbd>S</Kbd>, the sidebar row, the panel, the pill and the command. In a layout of your own, put `SchedulingProvider` inside `CalendarProvider`, around everything that reads it, and call `useSchedulingShortcuts()` before `useCalendarShortcuts()`, so <Kbd>Escape</Kbd> and <Kbd>Delete</Kbd> act on the marked times first:

```tsx
import { useCalendarShortcuts } from "@/hooks/use-calendar-shortcuts";
import { AvailabilityHint } from "@/components/availability-panel";
import { CalendarProvider } from "@/components/calendar-provider";
import { DetailsSidebar } from "@/components/details-sidebar";
import {
  SchedulingProvider,
  useSchedulingShortcuts,
} from "@/components/scheduling-provider";
import { SidebarProvider } from "@/components/ui/sidebar";
import { WeekView } from "@/components/week-view";

export function Schedule() {
  return (
    <CalendarProvider defaultEvents={events}>
      <SchedulingProvider>
        <ScheduleLayout />
      </SchedulingProvider>
    </CalendarProvider>
  );
}

function ScheduleLayout() {
  useSchedulingShortcuts();
  useCalendarShortcuts();

  return (
    <SidebarProvider>
      <div className="relative flex-1">
        <WeekView />
        <AvailabilityHint />
      </div>
      <DetailsSidebar />
    </SidebarProvider>
  );
}
```

The views, the sidebars and the command menu leave scheduling out when there's no `SchedulingProvider` around them. To mark times without the details sidebar, render `AvailabilityPanel` wherever your app has room, or pass `showActions` to `AvailabilityHint` for its Cancel and Copy buttons. Start marking from your own button with `useScheduling().startPicking()`.

## Scheduling links

Notion Calendar also creates scheduling links: booking pages where people pick one of your times. CalendarCN has no backend for them, so it hands them to your app. Pass `onCreateSchedulingLink` to `CalendarApp` (or `SchedulingProvider`), and the Scheduling row opens a menu with **Create recurring link** and **Create one-off link**, and the command menu lists "Create recurring scheduling link..." and "Create one-off scheduling link...". Without it they aren't offered, as in the live demo.

- **Recurring link**: your handler is called right away, with `kind: "recurring"` and the meeting length. Ask for the weekly hours in a form of your own.
- **One-off link**: the panel opens as "One-off link", you mark the times, and **Create** calls your handler with `kind: "one-off"` and the marked times, merged and in order.

```tsx
import { enUS } from "date-fns/locale";

import { formatAvailabilityText } from "@/lib/availability";

<CalendarApp
  defaultEvents={events}
  onCreateSchedulingLink={async (request) => {
    // request: { kind, durationMinutes, ranges, timeZone }
    const { url } = await createBookingPage(request);
    if (request.kind === "recurring") return;
    // Notion Calendar's text, ending with the booking page
    const text = formatAvailabilityText(request.ranges, {
      durationMinutes: request.durationMinutes,
      hourCycle: 12,
      locale: enUS,
      link: url,
    });
    await navigator.clipboard.writeText(text);
  }}
/>;
```

With `link`, the text ends with a blank line and "You can just let me know or confirm here: ..." and the link, as Notion Calendar's does.

## Keyboard shortcuts

| Action                          | Shortcut                          |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Start marking times             | <Kbd>S</Kbd>                      |
| Leave without copying           | <Kbd>Esc</Kbd>                    |
| Remove the selected marked time | <Kbd>Delete</Kbd> or <Kbd>⌫</Kbd> |

`useSchedulingShortcuts({ enabled: false })` turns them off; Calendar App does so with `shortcuts={false}`.

### formatAvailabilityText()

`formatAvailabilityText(ranges, options)` from `@/lib/availability` returns the text for any marked times, merged first. `options` takes `durationMinutes`, `hourCycle`, `locale`, and optionally `now` (for "today" and "tomorrow") and `link`.
