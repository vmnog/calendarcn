title: Time zones
description: Draw the calendar in a zone of your choice, show other zones' hours beside it, and travel to a zone for a while.

All of this goes in `<CalendarProvider>`'s props: see [Installation](/docs/reference/api#installation).

## Time zone

<ComponentPreview name="time-zone-demo" />

By default the calendar draws in each viewer's own time zone. `timeZone` takes an IANA zone, like `"Asia/Tokyo"`, and draws the calendar on that zone's clock, as Notion Calendar's primary time zone: a team calendar in the office's zone, or a page your server renders the same for every visitor. Events sit at that zone's time, "today", the current-time line and the first day of each week follow it, a double-click on an empty slot starts the event at that row's time there, the details show times in it, and the time axis is labeled with it. In the preview the calendar is in Tokyo, with India's hours beside it, and "Your clock" lists the same events on your own clock.

```tsx
<CalendarProvider
  events={events}
  onEventsChange={setEvents}
  timeZone="Asia/Tokyo"
>
  <WeekView />
</CalendarProvider>
```

- What you pass in and get back stays instants: `events`, `onEventsChange`, `onEventCreate`, `onEventUpdate` and the range `createEvent` gets. `onVisibleRangeChange` gets the instants the days on screen start and end in that zone. Save and fetch as you do without it.
- Inside the provider, times read on the calendar's clock. Events from `useCalendar()`, `renderEventContent` and `renderEventDetails` are `Date`s whose local time (`getHours()`, date-fns `format()`) is that zone's, and `updateEvent` takes them the same way. `fromZonedWallClock(date, timeZone)` from `@/lib/time-zones`, with `timeZone` from `useCalendarConfig()`, turns one back into an instant. To expand repeating events yourself, pass that `timeZone` to `expandRecurringEvents()`.
- Days are calendar dates: `date`, `currentDate`, `visibleDays`, `useCalendar().visibleRange` and all-day events are local midnights whose year, month and day name the day.
- An event keeps its own `timeZone`, and one without it stays in the viewer's: a repeating meeting keeps its clock time there, and the details' time zone row shows that zone.
- `timeZone` is the primary zone, so it changes from the time axis labels and the command menu too: pass `onTimeZoneChange` with it, or `defaultTimeZone` to let the provider own it.
- A zone the runtime doesn't know is ignored, and the calendar draws in the viewer's.
- On the one night a year the viewer's own clocks skip an hour, a time in that hour can't be drawn: an event there is drawn from the hour after, and keeps its times when you edit something else.

## Additional time zones

<ComponentPreview name="secondary-time-zone-demo" />

`secondaryTimeZones` takes IANA time zones, like `["Asia/Kolkata"]`, and `secondaryTimeZone` one of them. The day and week views then show each zone's hours in a column left of the time axis, left to right, as Notion Calendar does with additional time zones. Each column has its zone's short label at the top, and the current-time line keeps its one time badge, in your column, as in Notion Calendar. A zone half an hour or 45 minutes off yours shows the minutes: beside your 10 AM, India's column reads 7:30 PM.

```tsx
<CalendarProvider defaultEvents={events} secondaryTimeZone="Asia/Kolkata">
  <WeekView />
</CalendarProvider>
```

People change the zones from the time axis, as in Notion Calendar:

- A click or right-click on a zone's label opens its menu: "Change time zone", "Make time zone primary" (or "Make time zone secondary" on the primary one) and "Remove time zone from list". The "+" left of "All-day" adds a zone, as the left-most column. Both lists search by city, zone name or offset ("+9", "GMT-3").
- The zones are provider state like `view`: pass `secondaryTimeZones` with `onSecondaryTimeZonesChange` to keep them in your app, or `defaultSecondaryTimeZones` to let the provider own them. `useCalendar().changeTimeZones()` changes them from your own controls, with the helpers in `@/lib/time-zones` (`addTimeZone`, `removeTimeZone`, `makeTimeZonePrimary`, `makeTimeZoneSecondary`, `replaceTimeZone`).

- The column shows one offset for every day on screen: the one at noon today while today is on screen, else at noon on the first day shown. In a week where either zone changes its clocks, it is off by that change on the days on the other side of it.
- Each column is 64px wide at every screen size, so on a phone the days get less room. To show them on wide screens only, pass the prop there.
- Screen readers read each zone's label once. Both columns' hours are hidden from them, like the time axis itself, since each event's label already reads its time.
- A zone the runtime doesn't know gets no column.
- [Calendar](/docs/components/calendar) and [Calendar App](/docs/components/calendar-app) take the props too. Calendar App's command menu adds one with "Show additional time zones...", and makes one primary or hides one with "Make time zone primary..." and "Hide time zone...".

## Travel to a time zone

Notion Calendar's "Travel to time zone" draws the calendar in another zone for a while, without changing your zones: the zone travelled to gets a column next to the days, filled in the primary color, with your home zone beside it. `useCalendar().travelToTimeZone(zone)` travels and `travelToTimeZone(null)` travels back; `travelTimeZone` is the zone travelled to, or `null` at home.

- In [Calendar App](/docs/components/calendar-app), <Kbd>Z</Kbd> or "Travel to time zone..." in the command menu lists the zones to travel to.
- The x on the travel label, "Travel back home" in its menu, and <Kbd>Esc</Kbd> with no event selected (with `useCalendarShortcuts()`) travel back. "Add as primary time zone" and "Add as secondary time zone" keep the zone.
- Travelling isn't saved: a reload starts at home.
